# LIMIT SET — a 4K intro for DB32

A 60-second flight through **hyperbolic space**, ray traced per pixel on the DB32 machine
(`dynamic-1`, packed with the kit's range coder: **4,074-byte payload**). One honeycomb
parameter is swept from flat Euclidean space, through regular hyperbolic honeycombs, past
infinity, until the space falls apart into the fractal **limit set** of its own symmetry
group. The finale leaves hyperbolic space and shows the whole universe from outside, as a
ball with the limit set drawn on its sphere at infinity. The soundtrack is original, 128 BPM
in D minor, and loops with the pictures.

## How it works

### The honeycomb tracer

All geometry lives in the **Klein model** of hyperbolic space: points are homogeneous
4-vectors under the Lorentz form ⟨a,b⟩ = aₓbₓ + a_yb_y + a_zb_z − a_wb_w, hyperbolic
geodesics are straight lines and hyperbolic planes are flat. The regular honeycomb {p,q,r}
is generated by reflections in the faces of one cell, so every ray **bounces inside a
single cell**. It leaves through the nearest face, and a Lorentz reflection in that face maps
the neighbouring cell back onto this one. Tracing is therefore a fixed sequence of
plane exits and linear reflections; no distance fields and no marching.

- A cell is |n_j · K| ≤ c for six face normals n_j (the cube pads with zero normals). The
  face offset c = tanh h follows from the half period h. The dihedral angle
  cos θ = (c² − n_a·n_b)/(1 − c²) gives c for any {p,q,r}: c = 0.486 for {4,3,5},
  c = 0.669 for {5,3,4}, and c = 1/√3 for {4,3,6}, whose vertices lie on the sphere at
  infinity.
- The state is kept in **face coordinates** k_j = n_j·K and s_j = n_j·D, two blocks of six
  registers. Exit: t_j = (c|s_j| − k_j s_j)/s_j², compared by cross-multiplication, so no
  division happens unless a face wins, and never by zero. Moving is two `vscale`/`vadd` pairs.
  The reflection is **s_j += m s_a (σ c k_j − G_aj)**, with m = 2/(1 − c²) and G the face
  normals' Gram matrix from a table (`cells.py`). The point stays on the face.
- **Edges** are where two faces meet: the second-smallest slack c − |k_j| at the exit point is
  the distance to an edge, drawn as a band with a rounded profile and a white specular core.
  Every crossing that misses adds a glow term, and a glass term that brightens at grazing
  angles and near infinity (the rims of the limit set's discs).
- **Fog is exact hyperbolic distance:** cosh L = (1 − K·K′)/√((1 − |K|²)(1 − |K′|²)) per
  segment, and e^−L = cosh L − sinh L. Rays die in the fog below 3% transmittance, which
  keeps the average bounce count low.
- **Orbs** at every cell centre (the origin of the folded cell) come from the closest approach
  of each segment, K·D, so all cells are lit.
- Past c = 1/√3 the cube's corners are *hyperideal*. A ray can reach |K| = 1 inside the cell,
  i.e. escape to infinity through a hole of the limit set; it takes the sky colour with a
  gradient towards the hole's centre. Past c = 1/√2 the faces are ultraparallel and the
  group is a Schottky group: nothing but nested discs.
- In between, non-integer r gives non-discrete groups. The tracer does not care, so the
  honeycomb grows smoothly from {4,3,4} to {4,3,5} in scene 1.

### Camera

The eye flies along the x axis through window centres, i.e. a hyperbolic translation (boost)
by distance d. With T = tanh d and S = √(1 − T²), the boosted eye and every ray are closed
forms: K₀ = (T, p₀y S, p₀z S) and D ∝ (vₓS, v_y − vₓT p₀y, v_z − vₓT p₀z). No cosh or
exp is needed; tanh is the Padé form x(15 + x²)/(15 + 6x²). The distance wraps every 2h.
Each wrap is a **screw**, a translation by 2h composed with a half turn about x: reflection in
the face ∘ point reflection through the centre, which is a symmetry of every centrally
symmetric cell. So the camera state is folded back with two sign flips. The wraps are timed
to land on beats; the path-dependent cell colours change there, on the kick. Look direction
and tilt are interpolated per scene.

### The limit set from outside

For the finale the eye sits outside the unit ball, and each ray's entry point on the sphere
at infinity is folded into the cube's chamber with the cube's own mirrors (abs, largest
coordinate to x). It is then reflected in the face x = c while beyond it:
x −= m(x − c), K /= 1 − m(x − c)c. The number of reflections is the depth of the disc the
point lies in. It colours the sphere, the distance to the face draws the disc rims, and points
that run out of iterations are the limit set itself. As c shrinks from 0.73 to 0.65 the
pattern closes from Cantor dust towards an Apollonian-like gasket.

### Frame pipeline

- **CPU:** an MMIO config script, then per frame:
  - Find the scene (frame / 450).
  - On a scene change, rebuild the 23-word parameter block. Scene records are **delta-coded**
    (a field mask plus the changed words), applied from scene 0 on.
  - Interpolate eleven [start | end] bf16 pairs into GPU uniforms. The linear interpolation
    works on the bit patterns, which makes it geometric, good for brightness and widths.
  - Dispatch the renderer in three 30-row strips plus one strip for the bottom letterbox bar
    (captions), then flip pages at vblank.
- **GPU (one kernel):**
  - camera → face coordinates → bounce loop (or the ball's fold loop);
  - palette (a cosine palette per cell hue), sky and haze;
  - exposure with a **beat pulse** (from the frame counter, 28.125 frames per beat);
  - fade and flash, text (5×5 font, centred, fading);
  - a temporal blend with the frame on screen (binding 3), which steadies sub-pixel far cells;
  - sqrt gamma, store.
  - Letterbox: 160×90 image in 160×120 pages.
- **SPU:** the data-driven FM/noise engine from MEGAMIX (stateless, taps for echoes, poly
  voices, chord-relative notes, sidechain ducking, soft limiter) with a new song: Dm | Bb | F | C,
  a syncopated lead hook, crystal ratio-3 FM arpeggios, a pad an octave up, a snare roll into the
  drop, and the Schottky section a whole step up.

### Size

The RAM image is 6,968 bytes. The range coder packs it to a 3,722-byte stream, and the
352-byte decoder brings the payload to 4,074 bytes. What made it fit:

- delta-coded scenes;
- the face-coordinate setup as a loop that shifts register blocks with overlapping `vscale`
  (the WebGPU compiler builds vector results in a temporary first);
- float literals rounded to short mantissas (`.round`/`.roundbf`, geometry marked exact);
- float loop counters, a Gram-row reflection instead of vector algebra;
- register renaming for the coder's byte lanes (`tools/mregopt.mjs`).

## Verification

- **Official validator** (`artifacts/limit/validation/report.json`): 3,600 frames (60 s) on
  the WASM reference, `ok: true`. No faults, no audio overruns, no clipped samples, audio
  peak 0.82, SPU at most 34,120 ticks per block (52% of the budget).
- **WASM reference:** a new frame about every 2.3 vblanks (≈26 fps). The heaviest 30-row strip
  is 21% of the 1M-tick dispatch budget.
- **WebGPU (browser viewer):** GPU and SPU both on WebGPU, host at 60 fps with a new frame every
  vblank, dispatches ≈0.4–0.8 ms, SPU block kernel ≈0.5 ms, no overruns.
- **WebGPU step limit (8,192 per invocation):**
  - GPU: worst pixel 2,433 instructions (`tools/msim.mjs`, all scenes).
  - SPU: worst sample 7,587 (`tools/mspu.mjs`, every bar, dense sampling on the heaviest bars).
